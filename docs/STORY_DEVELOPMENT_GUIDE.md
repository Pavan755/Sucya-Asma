# Story development guide

Use `templates/story-template.md` and follow the research-to-script sequence in `docs/PRODUCTION_WORKFLOW.md`.

1. **Question:** frame one question for one audience and a specific runtime.
2. **Source map:** list the texts, records, scholarship, community traditions, and open disagreements that bear on the question.
3. **Claim ledger:** break the argument into checkable claims; each has sources and a confidence/uncertainty note.
4. **Thesis:** answer the question in one sentence that does not exceed the evidence.
5. **Beats:** order the evidence and consequences so each scene changes what the viewer understands.
6. **Narration:** write for speech; attach claim IDs; mark inference, tradition and dramatization.
7. **Fact-check:** a reviewer checks every factual sentence, name, date and causal link against exact source locations.
8. **Visual plan:** assign shot IDs; decide whether each image is evidence, illustration, reconstruction, map/diagram, or generated atmosphere.
9. **Approval:** save the approved script version and review record before final storyboards or generation.

## Adaptation rules

- Do not turn a devotional/traditional story into a claim about a historical event unless evidence independently supports that claim.
- Do not claim a passage appears in a named text without precise citation to a specific edition/translation.
- Identify composite or invented dialogue and avoid presenting it as quotation.
- Preserve meaningful variants; explain which version this episode follows.
- If evidence is uncertain, say so in script or narrow the claim.
- After a thesis change, re-check all script lines and shots associated with the former thesis.

Story quality includes clarity, emotional movement, cultural respect, and evidence fit. Suspense is created by a question and a sequence of discoveries, not by misleading the viewer.
