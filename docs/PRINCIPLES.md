# Principles

1. **Evidence before certainty.** Claims point to inspectable sources; disagreements and uncertainty stay visible.
2. **Name the tradition.** Identify the text, edition, historical evidence, oral/regional tradition, or adaptation being presented.
3. **Respect living communities.** Seek qualified perspectives on sensitive or contested material; avoid ridicule, sensationalism, and flattening regional differences.
4. **Separate evidence from imagery.** Label archival material, sourced art, maps, reconstructions, and generated assets.
5. **Version canon.** Approved character/environment traits are documented, not silently overwritten; major redesigns require approval.
6. **Automate repeatable work.** Automate cataloguing, file checks, draft assembly, and technical validation before automating interpretation or approval.
7. **Use human approval at meaningful gates.** Source framing, thesis, script, character design, storyboard, final cut, and release require a person.
8. **Keep provenance.** Every research claim and production asset has an ID, origin, rights note, and episode use.
9. **Build continuity.** Keep characters, places, names, tone, and series facts coherent while recording tradition variants.
10. **Start small.** Prove one complete short episode before adding agents, tools, or a release schedule.
11. **Protect the archive.** Use clear paths, versions, backups, and open formats. Git stores code and text, not books or large raw media.
12. **Be transparent about AI.** Keep prompts/model details in production records; generated depictions are not historical evidence.
