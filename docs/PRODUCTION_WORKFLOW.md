# Production workflow

This is the canonical idea-to-release flow. `core/` specifications can later implement these steps. For now the workflow is human-directed and can run with folders, checklists, and ledgers.

## 0. Project setup

Create an episode folder from the template. Add an episode ID, version, owner, status, intended audience, working length, language, and storage paths. Confirm source/media licensing and available storage before collecting large files.

**Gate:** paths work; collaborators know which version is approved.

## 1. Ideation and selection

Write several one-sentence ideas. Record each central question, viewer takeaway, why it matters, source base, character opportunities, visual potential, sensitivity, and effort. Compare source strength, story clarity, audience relevance, visual potential, feasibility, and respectful treatment. Choose one bounded idea rather than a broad subject.

**Gate:** one question can be answered in the planned runtime using sources we can inspect.

## 2. Research and source ledger

Collect bibliographic records in Zotero. Create an episode source ledger with stable IDs, exact editions/URLs/pages, access date, source type, rights/usage notes, and reliability limits. Respect copyright: record citations and lawful notes; do not bulk upload or redistribute copyrighted books without permission.

Build a claim ledger. Each factual or interpretive statement has a claim ID, wording, source ID/location, corroboration, confidence, disagreement, intended use, and review state. Categorize material as text, historical evidence, living tradition, interpretation, or dramatization.

**Gate:** premise is accurately described, central claims have citations, and disagreements are visible.

## 3. Story development

Write the thesis and viewer question, then a beat outline, then narration. Each beat advances understanding or emotion. Attach claim IDs to factual lines and shot IDs to planned visuals. Mark composite scenes, invented dialogue, uncertain dates, and omissions. Fact-check every substantive revision against the ledgers.

**Gate:** an unfamiliar reader can restate the point; important statements are traceable; dramatization does not masquerade as evidence.

## 4. Characters and world

Use `docs/CHARACTER_WORKFLOW.md`. Keep the identity bible separate from performance, motion, reference images, and version approvals. Record tradition variants rather than forcing them into one “definitive” design.

**Gate:** creative lead approves a reference pack and versioned bible before production shots use it.

## 5. Director plan and storyboard

The director receives the approved script, claim/source ledger, character/environment canon, runtime, audience, and format. It proposes scenes, beats, shot IDs, framing, movement, duration, sound intent, asset type, evidence label, and continuity constraints. The user approves the plan. Rough sketches are enough before expensive generation.

**Gate:** every shot supports a beat, source status is visible, and the sequence is feasible.

## 6. Asset production

Use archival/public-domain/licensed materials, original illustrations, maps, diagrams, motion graphics, Blender scenes, or generated assets as the board requires. Keep original source assets untouched; version derivatives and record provenance. For AI assets, record model/checkpoint, software version, prompt, settings, date, and license. Inspect for historical/cultural errors and continuity.

Start with stills and simple motion. Generate short, controlled video shots only after still-frame continuity is approved. Do not present synthetic images as documentary evidence.

**Gate:** each asset is approved, linked to shot/claims, and has rights/provenance recorded.

## 7. Narration and sound

Record narration or use a licensed TTS option. Check pronunciation with credible references. Keep voice, ambience, music, and effects on separate tracks. Record audio source, creator, license, attribution, and restrictions. Review transcripts manually before captions.

**Gate:** clear voice, balanced mix, reviewed transcript, rights-cleared audio.

## 8. Edit and review

Assemble in Kdenlive using organized bins and stable paths. Make a rough cut; review story and timing before effects. Render a review copy, collect timecoded notes, then check claims, captions, continuity, generated artifacts, credits and audio again. Save editor project, media manifest, and export settings.

**Gate:** review notes are resolved or consciously accepted; the master plays start to end; captions and credits match.

## 9. Release and reuse

Prepare master, platform formats, captions, thumbnail, description, credits and social cutdowns. Give short clips enough context to stay accurate. Check platform specifications and rights shortly before upload. Archive editable project, final files, source/rights notes, prompts, manifests, and release notes in two locations.

**Gate:** human release approval and a tested archive copy.

## Pivoting an idea

If research contradicts the premise, sources are inaccessible, scope is too broad, ethics/rights cannot be resolved, or a stronger question emerges, pause downstream work. Record old and new thesis, evidence/constraint, what carries over, what must be rechecked, and why the pivot helps. Archive the old version; do not overwrite it. Re-audit every script line and shot tied to the old thesis.
